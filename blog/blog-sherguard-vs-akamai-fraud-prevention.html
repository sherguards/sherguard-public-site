SherGuard vs Akamai: Which Application Security and Fraud Prevention Platform Is Right for Your Business?

Compare SherGuard and Akamai across bot detection, fake signup prevention, device risk intelligence, API abuse, account takeover, payment fraud, application security, and protection for modern online businesses.

Introduction

Every business that operates online depends on systems that attackers may try to exploit. Websites, mobile applications, APIs, customer accounts, payment workflows, and digital services all create potential entry points for malicious activity.

Attackers may launch distributed denial-of-service attacks, automate account creation, steal credentials, scrape content, exploit application vulnerabilities, abuse APIs, or manipulate payment systems. Some attacks are designed to steal information. Others aim to disrupt services, commit fraud, consume resources, or damage customer trust.

These threats are connected, but they are not identical. A web application firewall can help protect against malicious requests and known attack patterns. Bot management can identify automated traffic. Account protection can identify suspicious account activity. Payment fraud detection can evaluate transaction risk.

Choosing a security platform therefore requires understanding exactly which risks the technology is designed to address.

Two platforms worth comparing are SherGuard and Akamai.

Akamai is a major cloud and cybersecurity provider with an extensive security portfolio. Its capabilities include web application and API protection, bot management, account protection, DDoS defense, and content protection. Its global edge infrastructure is an important part of its security architecture.

SherGuard takes a different approach. It is a Trust Intelligence Platform centered on five core detection areas: fake signup detection, device risk intelligence, bot detection, API abuse detection, and payment fraud detection.

This article examines the differences between the two platforms, explains where Akamai has established strengths, and identifies the business requirements for which SherGuard may be a more suitable option.

The objective is not to claim that one platform is universally better. It is to help businesses make a decision based on their security architecture, fraud exposure, application requirements, and operational needs.

What Is Akamai?

Akamai is a global cloud and cybersecurity company providing services for application delivery, cloud computing, infrastructure security, and digital experiences.

Its security portfolio includes several products designed to protect websites, applications, APIs, users, and digital services.

Important security products include:

  • App & API Protector: web application firewall and API protection.
  • Bot Manager: detection and mitigation of automated traffic.
  • Account Protector: protection against account takeover and account abuse.
  • Content Protector: protection against unauthorized content scraping and related abuse.
  • Prolexic: DDoS protection for infrastructure and applications.

These products address different security problems and can be used as part of a broader Akamai security deployment.

Official product information: Akamai Products.

How Akamai Protects Websites, Applications, and APIs

1. Global Edge Infrastructure

One of Akamai's major strengths is its distributed global infrastructure. Its edge network allows services to be delivered and security controls to be applied closer to users and incoming traffic.

This architecture can help organizations manage traffic, reduce exposure to malicious requests, and maintain service availability during periods of high demand or attack.

For large organizations operating across multiple regions, global infrastructure can be an important part of application performance and security strategy.

2. Web Application Firewall

A web application firewall, or WAF, examines HTTP and HTTPS traffic and applies security rules to identify and block malicious requests.

WAF protection can help defend against common application attacks, suspicious request patterns, and attempts to exploit known vulnerabilities.

Akamai's App & API Protector combines web application and API security capabilities. It is designed to protect application traffic and reduce exposure to attacks targeting web applications and APIs.

Official product information: Akamai App & API Protector.

3. Bot Manager

Akamai Bot Manager is designed to identify and mitigate automated traffic, including malicious bots that interact with websites, mobile applications, and APIs.

Bot attacks may involve scraping, account takeover attempts, automated purchases, inventory abuse, credential stuffing, and other activities that affect business operations.

Akamai describes its bot detection approach as using behavioral analysis, device signals, and other detection techniques to classify traffic and apply appropriate responses.

Official product information: Akamai Bot Manager.

4. Account Protector

Account takeover is a major risk for digital businesses. Attackers may use stolen credentials, credential stuffing, phishing, or automated login attempts to gain access to legitimate accounts.

Akamai Account Protector is designed to identify suspicious account activity and protect digital account workflows.

Its approach includes evaluating signals associated with users, devices, and behavior to help identify account abuse and potential compromise.

Official product information: Akamai Account Protector.

5. Content Protection

Unauthorized scraping can affect businesses that rely on proprietary content, pricing information, product catalogs, inventory, or valuable digital data.

Akamai Content Protector is designed to help identify and mitigate automated content scraping and related abuse.

This can be particularly important for organizations whose content or inventory information has commercial value.

6. DDoS Protection

Distributed denial-of-service attacks attempt to overwhelm a target with traffic or requests, disrupting access to websites, applications, or infrastructure.

Akamai offers DDoS protection through its security portfolio, including Prolexic services designed to protect network infrastructure and maintain availability during attacks.

This is an important distinction because DDoS mitigation addresses availability threats that are not the same as fake account creation, device risk, or payment fraud.

How to Choose Between SherGuard and Akamai

Step 1: Identify Your Main Security Problems

Determine whether your most important risks are DDoS, application attacks, bot traffic, fake accounts, account takeover, API abuse, scraping, or payment fraud.

Different risks require different controls. A platform should be evaluated against the problems that matter most to your organization.

Step 2: Separate Infrastructure Security From Trust Intelligence

Infrastructure security protects availability, networks, and application entry points. Trust intelligence evaluates whether digital interactions and business activity appear legitimate or risky.

These functions complement each other, but they are not interchangeable.

Step 3: Map Your Customer Journey

Review how users interact with your business:

  1. A visitor arrives at your website or application.
  2. The visitor creates an account.
  3. The user authenticates.
  4. The user interacts with your application.
  5. The user accesses APIs.
  6. The user makes a payment.
  7. The user continues using your service.

Identify the security controls required at each stage.

Step 4: Test Realistic Attack Scenarios

Use controlled testing to evaluate:

  • Automated account registrations.
  • Credential stuffing attempts.
  • Suspicious device patterns.
  • Unusual API request behavior.
  • Automated payment attempts.
  • Content scraping.
  • Application-layer attacks.
  • DDoS resilience.

Step 5: Measure Results

Evaluate detection quality, false positives, response times, application latency, operational workload, integration complexity, and the business impact of prevented attacks.
