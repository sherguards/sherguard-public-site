SherGuard vs Forter: Which Fraud Prevention Platform Is Right for Your Business?

Compare two approaches to digital trust and fraud prevention. Explore Forter's commerce-focused fraud decisioning alongside SherGuard's connected intelligence for fake signups, device risk, bot activity, API abuse, and payment fraud.

Introduction: Fraud Prevention Is About More Than Declining Suspicious Payments

Digital businesses face fraud throughout the customer journey. A fraudulent event can begin when an attacker creates a fake account, continues when that account accesses a service through an unusual device, develops into automated activity against an API, and eventually results in a suspicious payment or abuse of a promotion.

For e-commerce businesses, marketplaces, fintech companies, and subscription services, these events can create financial losses, chargebacks, operational costs, customer complaints, and damage to brand reputation. Fraud can also affect legitimate customers. Overly aggressive security controls may reject genuine transactions, block valid accounts, and introduce unnecessary friction into checkout.

This creates a difficult balance: businesses must prevent harmful activity without making it unnecessarily difficult for legitimate customers to register, log in, make purchases, or use digital services.

Two platforms that businesses may consider when evaluating these challenges are Forter and SherGuard. Both operate in the broader digital trust and fraud prevention space, but their product positioning and areas of emphasis differ.

Forter positions its platform around commerce protection and fraud decisioning. Its published product portfolio covers areas such as payment fraud prevention, account protection, abuse prevention, and chargeback-related risk. It describes the use of identity, behavioral, device, and transaction signals to support decisions across digital commerce.

SherGuard is positioned as a Trust Intelligence Platform. It combines five core modules: Email Risk Intelligence, Device Risk Intelligence, Bot Detection Intelligence, API Abuse Intelligence, and Payment Fraud Intelligence. Its purpose is to help businesses evaluate suspicious activity across registration, device, automated behavior, API usage, and payment workflows.

This comparison examines their different approaches, the business problems they address, and the questions buyers should ask before selecting a platform. It does not assume that either provider is universally superior or that similarly named features have identical technical capabilities.

4. Understanding the Different Fraud Problems

The term fraud prevention covers several distinct problems. A business may need to stop unauthorized payments, prevent fake registrations, identify account takeover, reduce promotional abuse, detect automated traffic, or protect APIs from misuse.

These risks can be connected, but they are not interchangeable. A system that detects suspicious payments may not automatically identify a vulnerable API. A bot detection system may identify automated traffic without determining whether a transaction is financially fraudulent.

Payment fraud

Payment fraud involves unauthorized or deceptive payment activity. Examples include stolen card use, card testing, compromised accounts, and suspicious transactions.

Account fraud

Account fraud involves the creation, compromise, or misuse of online accounts. It can include fake account creation, credential stuffing, account takeover, and abuse of account privileges.

Automated abuse

Automated abuse involves scripts, bots, or other automated clients performing harmful actions. Examples include scraping, fake registrations, repeated checkout attempts, promotional abuse, and excessive API requests.

API abuse and vulnerabilities

API abuse involves using API endpoints in suspicious or harmful ways. API vulnerabilities involve weaknesses in the implementation or security design of the endpoints. Businesses may need separate controls to identify both.

Why connected intelligence matters

An attacker may use several of these methods in sequence. For example, automated registration may create fake accounts, suspicious devices may be reused, APIs may be accessed repeatedly, and payment attempts may follow.

If a business reviews each event independently, it may miss the broader pattern. Connected signals can help teams investigate activity across multiple stages of the customer journey.

5. Real-World Attack Scenarios: Comparing the Approaches

Scenario 1: Fake accounts created to exploit free trials

A SaaS company offers a free trial to new customers. An attacker writes an automated script that creates hundreds of accounts using disposable email addresses and slightly different registration details.

The attacker may use the accounts to consume resources, obtain repeated trial access, claim referral rewards, or prepare accounts for later misuse.

Useful defenses include email risk analysis, device context, bot detection, registration velocity controls, and additional verification. SherGuard's Email Risk Intelligence, Device Risk Intelligence, and Bot Detection Intelligence are relevant to these risk areas.

Forter's account protection and identity-related capabilities may be relevant to account abuse in commerce environments. Buyers should verify the precise fake signup and registration controls available in the product configuration they are considering.

Scenario 2: Stolen payment credentials used for purchases

An attacker obtains payment card details and uses them to place orders through an online store. The attacker may use different accounts, devices, delivery addresses, or transaction amounts to avoid basic fraud rules.

The business must decide whether the transaction is legitimate before fulfilling the order. Incorrect approval can result in financial loss and disputes. Incorrect rejection can prevent a genuine customer from completing a purchase.

Forter's payment fraud prevention capabilities are directly relevant to this commerce decisioning problem. SherGuard's Payment Fraud Intelligence is designed to provide additional payment risk signals, potentially considered alongside device and bot information.

Businesses should test both platforms against representative transaction patterns and evaluate actual approval quality, fraud outcomes, false declines, latency, and integration effort.

Scenario 3: Account takeover followed by fraudulent purchases

An attacker uses stolen credentials to access a customer's account. After gaining access, the attacker changes account information, uses stored payment details, or places orders using the customer's identity.

Account takeover can be difficult to detect because the attacker may use valid credentials and an existing customer account.

Forter's dedicated account protection capabilities are relevant to this threat. SherGuard's device, bot, email, and API risk signals may contribute to suspicious access analysis when integrated into the relevant workflows.

A complete defense should include multifactor authentication, breached-password controls, secure session management, account recovery protection, anomaly detection, and transaction monitoring.

Scenario 4: Automated card testing

An attacker sends repeated payment attempts through a checkout form to determine which card details are valid. The attempts may use small amounts, multiple accounts, and changing client information.

This can create processor costs, declined transactions, fraud alerts, and disruption to legitimate checkout activity.

SherGuard's Payment Fraud Intelligence and Bot Detection Intelligence are relevant to analyzing suspicious payment patterns and automation-related signals. Forter's commerce fraud capabilities should also be evaluated for the business's specific card testing and transaction abuse requirements.

Rate limits, processor controls, transaction velocity rules, and appropriate verification remain important complementary defenses.

Scenario 5: Promotion and refund abuse

A retailer offers discounts to new customers and allows refunds under defined conditions. An organized group creates multiple accounts to claim new-customer promotions repeatedly or submits suspicious refund requests.

These activities may not always involve stolen payment credentials. They can instead exploit legitimate business policies.

Forter's commerce-focused abuse prevention capabilities are relevant to this type of problem. SherGuard may help identify suspicious account creation, device patterns, automated behavior, and payment-related risk signals that could contribute to an investigation.

Businesses should distinguish between malicious abuse and legitimate customers who are using promotions or requesting refunds within the published terms.

Scenario 6: API scraping and automated data collection

A marketplace exposes APIs used by its website and mobile applications. An attacker identifies an endpoint that returns product, seller, or listing information and sends repeated requests to collect data at scale.

The requests may be valid at the HTTP level. The problem is the pattern and purpose of the activity.

SherGuard API Abuse Intelligence is designed to help identify suspicious usage patterns such as bursts and repeated endpoint requests. Bot Detection Intelligence may provide additional automation context.

Forter is primarily positioned around commerce fraud and trust decisions. Businesses that need dedicated API discovery, vulnerability assessment, or broad API threat protection should evaluate a specialist API security product as well.

7. Detailed Comparison: Fake Accounts and Account Protection

Fake account creation and account takeover are different problems, but both can undermine customer trust.

A fake account is created using false, disposable, or misleading information. An account takeover involves unauthorized access to an existing legitimate account. Businesses should have controls for both.

Fake account creation

Fake accounts can be used to exploit free trials, referral programs, promotional offers, marketplace listings, loyalty benefits, and platform resources.

Useful signals include email reputation, signup velocity, device patterns, automated behavior, network context, and repeated registration characteristics.

SherGuard's Email Risk Intelligence, Device Risk Intelligence, and Bot Detection Intelligence are relevant to these signup risk areas. Forter's account protection and identity intelligence capabilities may also be relevant in digital commerce environments.

Account takeover

Account takeover usually involves an attacker accessing an existing account through stolen credentials, compromised sessions, social engineering, or other methods.

Forter offers dedicated account protection capabilities. Businesses should examine the supported attack types, login events, account signals, response options, and integrations.

SherGuard's device, bot, email, and API intelligence may help identify suspicious activity surrounding an account, but businesses should not assume that these modules provide every function of a dedicated account takeover solution.

Why the distinction matters

Preventing fake registrations requires evaluating new account creation. Preventing account takeover requires protecting existing identities and sessions. The same device or bot signal may be useful in both workflows, but the decisions and evidence requirements are different.

  • Automated requests designed to exhaust service resources.
  • Attempts to enumerate account or object identifiers.
  • Repeated calls against login, registration, or checkout endpoints.
  • Misuse of valid API credentials.
  • Abusive access to business workflows through public endpoints.

SherGuard API Abuse Intelligence

Why API abuse detection is not API vulnerability testing

An API may be vulnerable because it fails to enforce authorization correctly. Another API may be securely implemented but still receive excessive requests from an abusive client.

These are different problems. Businesses need secure API development and testing to identify vulnerabilities, alongside traffic monitoring and abuse detection to identify harmful usage patterns.

14. Limitations and Important Security Considerations

No fraud prevention platform can guarantee that every fraudulent transaction, fake account, compromised identity, or abusive request will be detected. Attackers change their methods, legitimate customer behavior varies, and risk signals are not always conclusive.

What Forter should not be assumed to replace

A commerce fraud platform does not automatically replace secure software development, API vulnerability testing, web application firewalls, DDoS protection, endpoint security, identity governance, backups, or incident response.

Organizations should confirm the exact product scope and integrations included in their Forter proposal. They should distinguish payment decisioning, account protection, abuse prevention, and chargeback-related services rather than assuming that every use case is handled in the same way.

What SherGuard should not be assumed to replace

SherGuard's five intelligence modules are designed to evaluate signup, device, bot, API abuse, and payment risk. They should not be treated as a complete replacement for dedicated payment decisioning networks, a full account takeover platform, a WAF, DDoS protection, API vulnerability management, secure development, or a security operations program.

Businesses should test the actual detection outputs, available data, integrations, and enforcement options before depending on the platform for production decisions.

False positives and customer experience

A legitimate customer may use a VPN, share a device, travel, change payment methods, or behave differently from previous sessions. A single unusual signal should not automatically be treated as proof of fraud.

Businesses should establish risk thresholds, review procedures, and escalation paths. Where appropriate, additional verification may be better than immediate rejection.

Privacy and responsible data handling

Fraud detection often involves personal, device, behavioral, and transaction-related information. Businesses must determine what data they need, why it is collected, how long it is retained, who can access it, and which legal requirements apply.

Data minimization, access control, encryption, audit logging, and appropriate privacy disclosures should be part of the implementation plan.

15. How to Evaluate Both Platforms Before Making a Decision

A structured evaluation is more useful than choosing a provider based only on marketing claims. Businesses should define their risks, test representative activity, and measure the impact on both fraud and legitimate customers.

Step 1: Identify the biggest business risks

Determine whether the most serious problems involve unauthorized payments, fake accounts, account takeover, promotional abuse, suspicious devices, automated checkout, or API misuse.

Step 2: Map the customer journey

Document registration, login, browsing, account changes, checkout, payment, fulfillment, refunds, and customer support. Identify where suspicious activity can enter and where a risk decision is needed.

Step 3: Match requirements to product capabilities

Compare Forter's relevant commerce protection products with SherGuard's five modules. Ask each provider to demonstrate the exact workflow required rather than relying on broad product descriptions.

Step 4: Test realistic fraud scenarios

Include suspicious registrations, repeated payment attempts, unusual devices, automated checkout, account compromise simulations, and abusive API traffic. Use controlled testing and follow the security policies of the environment.

Step 5: Measure detection and customer impact

Evaluate detection quality, false positives, false declines, latency, investigation time, operational workload, and integration complexity. A solution that blocks more activity is not necessarily better if it also rejects a large number of legitimate customers.

Step 6: Introduce enforcement carefully

Begin with monitoring where possible. Review the results, tune thresholds, and introduce verification or blocking for clearly defined risk conditions. Maintain a process for handling legitimate customers who are incorrectly flagged.

Step 7: Review ongoing ownership

Confirm who will manage integrations, monitor alerts, review fraud outcomes, update policies, and respond to incidents. Fraud prevention requires ongoing operational attention.
