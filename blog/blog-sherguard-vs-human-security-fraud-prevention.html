A detailed comparison of SherGuard and HUMAN Security covering bot detection, fake accounts, device risk, account takeover, API abuse, payment fraud, AI-agent traffic, and protection for modern online businesses.

Introduction

Online businesses increasingly depend on digital accounts, APIs, automated transactions, mobile applications, and interconnected services. These systems create opportunities for growth, but they also give attackers more ways to exploit a business.

Fraud is no longer limited to someone using a stolen credit card. An attack may begin with automated account creation, continue through credential abuse, involve suspicious devices, exploit application APIs, and eventually produce fraudulent transactions or account manipulation.

Some attacks are performed by automated bots. Others involve human operators, compromised accounts, organized fraud groups, or increasingly sophisticated AI-assisted activity. In many cases, several methods are combined.

This creates an important challenge for security teams: protecting one part of an application does not necessarily protect the entire customer journey. A business may have bot protection but still experience payment fraud. It may have payment controls but remain vulnerable to fake account creation or API abuse.

Two platforms worth examining in this environment are SherGuard and HUMAN Security.

HUMAN Security operates a broad cybersecurity platform designed to detect and mitigate automated threats, human-led fraud, and risky AI-agent activity. Its application protection capabilities address use cases including bot detection, account takeover, fake accounts, scraping, and transaction abuse. Its platform also includes client-side security capabilities.

SherGuard takes a trust-intelligence approach built around five core detection areas: fake signup detection, device risk intelligence, bot detection, API abuse detection, and payment fraud detection.

Both platforms address important digital trust problems, but their product structures, capabilities, and positioning are not identical.

This comparison examines where HUMAN has established strengths, where SherGuard offers a different approach, and which type of organization may benefit from evaluating each platform.

Why Businesses Need More Than Basic Bot Protection

Many organizations initially approach cybersecurity by purchasing tools for individual problems. They might install a web application firewall, introduce CAPTCHA challenges, add payment fraud rules, or implement rate limiting on selected API endpoints.

These controls can be valuable, but attackers often move between different parts of an application. A defense that protects one endpoint may not recognize suspicious activity elsewhere.

Example: A Coordinated Fake Account Attack

Consider an online marketplace that offers promotional credits to new users. An attacker may use automated scripts to create hundreds or thousands of accounts. Those accounts may then be used to claim promotions, manipulate listings, send spam, or purchase products using fraudulent payment methods.

If the marketplace only monitors payment transactions, it may detect the final fraudulent purchase but miss the earlier account creation activity. If it only blocks obvious bots, human-assisted account creation may still succeed.

A stronger approach considers multiple signals:

  • Whether registration activity appears suspicious.
  • Whether multiple accounts are associated with risky devices.
  • Whether behavior resembles automation.
  • Whether API requests follow abnormal patterns.
  • Whether payment activity contains fraud indicators.
  • Whether suspicious events are connected across the customer journey.

This is the broader problem that trust intelligence and modern fraud prevention platforms are intended to address.

False Positives and the Customer Experience

Fraud prevention systems must distinguish between legitimate customers and suspicious activity. Blocking malicious traffic is important, but incorrectly blocking genuine users can also damage a business.

A false positive may prevent a customer from registering, accessing an account, using a mobile application, or completing a purchase. Repeated friction can lead to abandoned transactions and customer dissatisfaction.

HUMAN emphasizes decision precision and mitigation approaches intended to reduce unnecessary friction for legitimate users. Its platform supports different response options, including blocking and softer mitigation actions.

SherGuard's multi-module approach gives businesses separate categories of risk to evaluate. This can help avoid treating every suspicious event as the same type of problem.

However, no detection platform should be assumed to eliminate false positives. Organizations need to test their own traffic, monitor decisions, review suspicious cases, and adjust policies as their applications evolve.

How to Evaluate SherGuard and HUMAN Security

The best way to compare fraud prevention platforms is to test them against the attacks and workflows that matter to your organization.

Step 1: Identify Your Most Expensive Fraud Problems

Determine whether your largest losses come from fake accounts, account takeover, bot traffic, payment fraud, API abuse, scraping, or human-assisted fraud.

A platform should be evaluated against the problems that create measurable business exposure.

Step 2: Map Your Customer Journey

Review the full lifecycle of a customer:

  1. A visitor arrives at the website or mobile application.
  2. The visitor creates an account.
  3. The user authenticates.
  4. The user accesses application features.
  5. The user interacts with APIs.
  6. The user makes a payment or transaction.
  7. The account continues to perform activity.

Identify where suspicious activity can occur and which security controls should operate at each stage.

Step 3: Test Realistic Attack Scenarios

Use controlled tests to evaluate how each platform responds to:

  • Repeated suspicious account registrations.
  • Automated login attempts.
  • Suspicious device patterns.
  • Unusual API request behavior.
  • Automated payment attempts.
  • Scraping and excessive resource consumption.
  • Suspicious account activity after login.
  • Human-assisted fraud scenarios.
  • Unauthorized AI-agent activity.

Step 4: Measure Detection Quality

Do not evaluate a platform only by counting how many requests it blocks. Measure whether it correctly identifies malicious activity while allowing legitimate customers to continue.

Important metrics include:

  • Detection precision and recall.
  • False-positive and false-negative rates.
  • Time required to detect suspicious activity.
  • Latency added to application workflows.
  • Fraud losses prevented.
  • Investigation time saved.
  • Legitimate customer conversion impact.

Step 5: Evaluate Operational Requirements

A platform should fit the organization's engineering and security operations. Review deployment complexity, reporting, integrations, support, incident response, scalability, and the ability to adjust protection policies.
